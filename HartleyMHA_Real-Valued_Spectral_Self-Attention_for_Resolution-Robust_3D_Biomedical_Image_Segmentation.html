HartleyMHA: Real-Valued Spectral Self-Attention for Resolution-Robust 3D Biomedical Image Segmentation

    Supervisor: Vannary Meas-Yedid
    Contact: vmeasyed@pasteur.fr

    Description:

    The high computational demands of 3D biomedical image segmentation frequently cause out-of-memory errors in deep learning framework. To mitigate this, training with downsampled images is a common practice. However, this can lead to reduced accuracy when applying the model on original resolution images due to sensitivity to variations in image resolution.
    Since the introduction of Transformers, attention-based models have achieved promising results in image segmentation. However, the quadratic complexity of self-attention in image size becomes prohibitive in 3D: to avoid out-of-memory errors, models are typically trained on reduced input sizes, yielding suboptimal accuracy when applied at the original resolution. Inspired by the Fourier neural operator [1] (FNO); a framework for learning mappings between functions that offers a global receptive field and zero-shot super-resolution, the article [2] proposes HartleyMHA, a frequency-domain self-attention model robust to training resolution. By replacing the Fourier transform with a parameter-shared Hartley transform, the authors reduce the model size by orders of magnitude, enabling efficient self-attention for expressive high-order feature combination.

    This project focuses on efficient 3D image segmentation in order to segment volumetric electron microscope images. After a review of the existing models in 3D segmentation, the student will implement the HartleyMHA and make a comparison with existing methods, including the FNOSeg3D.

    The public Lucchi datasets (EPFL Hippocampus mitochondria segmentation) can be downloaded: https://www.epfl.ch/labs/cvlab/data/data-em/
