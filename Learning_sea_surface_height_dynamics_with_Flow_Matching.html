Learning sea surface height dynamics with Flow Matching

    Supervisor: Dominique Béréziat
    Contact: dominique.bereziat@lip6.fr

    Description:

    NATL60 is a realistic dataset simulating the North Atlantic ocean surface. It is
    provided by the Copernicus Marine Service [1]. The dataset contains 10 years of
    daily dates from 2007 to 2017, with a spatial resolution of 1/12 degree. The
    dataset includes variables such as sea surface temperature, salinity, currents,
    and sea level. NATL60 dataset will be provided by the supervisor.

    The goal of these project is to analyze the NATL60 dataset to understand the
    dynamics of the North Atlantic ocean surface. For this purpose, we propose to
    train a generative model to learn the probability distribution of
    a given variable conditionnaly to a date. In this project we focuse on SSH (Sea
    Surface Height) variable.

    Students should be able to produce a state-of-the-art review on generative
    models with a focus on Flow Matching [2], the method that we will use to train our
    model. Alternative methods can also be considered, for instance a variational
    autoencoder (VAE) as a baseline.

    In a second step, they should be able to implement a Flow Matching model and
    train it on the NATL60 dataset. Finally, they should be able to evaluate the
    performance of their model and analyze the results.

    In a third step, we plan to train this model to produce a forecast of the SSH
    variable at a short time horizon. Again, students should be able to evaluate the
    performance of their model and analyze the results.

    [1] https://marine.copernicus.eu/
    [2] Flow Matching for Generative Modeling, Lipman et al, in NeurIPS 2023.
