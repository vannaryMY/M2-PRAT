Lightweight Yet Global: A KAN–Mamba Hybrid for Biomedical Image Segmentation

    Supervisor: Vannary Meas-Yedid
    Contact: vmeasyed@pasteur.fr

    Description:

    Biomedical image segmentation has traditionally relied on CNNs and Transformers. However, CNNs are limited by small receptive fields, while Transformers scale poorly due to quadratic computational complexity. Two recent architectures address these issues: Mamba, a selective state-space model that captures long-range dependencies with near-linear complexity, and the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN), which enhances nonlinear expressiveness by replacing fixed activation functions with learnable ones. Their complementary strengths (efficient global context modeling and flexible non linear representation) make a KAN-Mamba hybrid a compelling candidate for accurate and memory-efficient image segmentation.

    Following a literature review of KAN- and Mamba- based approaches to image segmentation, the student will implement their own architecture, building on the following papers [1,2] and benchmark it against existing methods on public biomedical datasets (e.g, in term of Dice score, parameter numbers and memory footprint). Depending on progress, the work may be extended with ablation studies of the hybridization strategy.

    [1] Liu, J., Wu, J., Xu, L., Shi, W., & Zheng, B. (2026). A parallel UNet integrating KAN and mamba for medical image segmentation. Scientific Reports, 16(1), 13579.

    [2] Zhu, H., Chen, J., & He, G. (2026). Medvkan: Efficient feature extraction with mamba and kan for medical image segmentation. Biomedical Signal Processing and Control, 112, 108821.
