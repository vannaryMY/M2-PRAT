Segmentation and classification of cerebellar neurons in neurodegenerative disease

    Supervisor: Vadori Valentina
    Contact: valentina.vadori@pasteur.fr

    Description:

    Neurodegenerative disorders, such as lysosomal storage diseases (LSDs) and Alzheimer's disease, are a group of severe diseases affecting humans and animals and characterised by neuronal loss in several areas of the central nervous system, including the cerebellar cortex and, in particular, Purkinje and granule cells. Neuropathological assessment of neuronal loss is still often based on qualitative or semi-quantitative evaluation, whereas automated detection and quantification in histological sections could provide more objective and reproducible measurements. This requires models that can both segment and classify neuronal populations and remain reliable in diseased tissue, where cell morphology and tissue organisation may be altered. This project relies on a unique dataset of histological images of the cat cerebellum from control animals and cats affected by neuronal ceroid lipofuscinoses (NCL), in which granule and Purkinje cells were manually annotated by board-certified pathologists at the University of Padova. The objective is to assess how accurately state-of-the-art deep learning approaches can segment and classify these two neuronal populations, and whether their performance remains stable in diseased compared with control tissue. The study will compare representative approaches to cell instance segmentation and classification. These may include generalist segmentation foundation models with strong cross-domain generalisation, such as Cellpose-SAM, and histopathology-specific architectures that jointly address instance segmentation and cell classification, such as CellViT. Cellpose-SAM provides a strong general-purpose instance-segmentation baseline and can be integrated with an additional classification strategy if required. CellViT represents a complementary strategy in which instance segmentation and cell-type classification are integrated within a unified histopathology framework. The exact models and adaptation strategies will be selected during the project according to the characteristics of the available annotations, the bibliographic study and computational requirements. Evaluation will include instance segmentation, cell detection and class-specific classification metrics, with performance analysed separately in control and NCL tissue. This will allow the study to determine not only overall accuracy, but also whether disease-associated morphological alterations produce systematic performance degradation or different failure modes across neuronal populations. Zero-shot performance will be evaluated where supported by the selected models, followed by supervised adaptation when appropriate. Following a bibliographic study of cell instance segmentation, foundation models and cell classification in histopathology, the student will select and adapt suitable methods to the annotated cerebellar dataset and perform a comparative analysis of segmentation, classification and robustness to disease-related morphological changes.

    Main activities
    - Conduct a targeted literature review of cell segmentation and classification methods.
    - Select and adapt suitable approaches to the cerebellar dataset.
    - Evaluate segmentation, detection and classification in control and NCL tissue.
    - Compare performance and failure modes across neuronal populations and conditions.
