Virtual CODEX Staining: Recovering Immune and Structural Kidney Markers from Routine H&E with Deep Learning

    Supervisor: Vannary Meas-Yedid
    Contact: vmeasye@pasteur.fr

    Description:

    Acute interstitial nephropathies (AIN) are a frequent cause of acute kidney injury, accounting for up to 30% of cases. It results from inflammation that attacks the kidney’s interstitial tissue and the tubules that line it, producing a microscopic picture of infiltrating immune cells and damaged tubules.
    In the routine, identifying the specific immune cells in an AIN biopsy is difficult because a single sample contains thousands of cells that look similar. We will exploit a cutting edge, high resolution multiplex imaging platform, Co Detection by Indexing (CODEX) to visualize multiple molecular markers in a single tissue section. CODEX enables single cell resolution imaging of complex tissues, revealing cellular composition, interactions, and architectural details.
    While multiplex immunohistochemistry can separate the cell types, the technique is expensive and not practical for every day pathology work. That motivates computational approaches that translate routine stains into virtual CODEX images.

    The student will conduct a literature review on style-transfer and image-to-image translation models for microscopy data (virtual staining, in silico multiplexing). The goal of the project is then to develop and evaluate an AI model [1] that translates routine H&E (hematoxylin and eosin) or PAS-stain slides into multiplexed CODEX images, assessing fidelity against matched groundtruth CODEX acquisitions. Particular attention should be devoted to the class imbalance across markers (e.g, markers expressed in only a small fraction of cells)

    [1] Balezo, G., Trullo, R., Planas, A. P., Decencière, E., & Walter, T. (2026). Miphei-vit: Multiplex immunofluorescence prediction from h&e images using vit foundation models. Computers in Biology and Medicine, 206, 111564.
